# Round 2 – Over/ Under (3 points each)

*This category is all about nuance in relatability – it’s not difficult to answer whether a person has 2 legs, but it’s far too difficult to answer if there are over or under 20 million grains of sand in a gallon of sand – it’s a bit too far from the human experience/scale. Things that make good over-unders:*

* *Ages of very well-known (by their faces+names) celebrities.*   
* *Slightly obscure science facts about relatable topics (i.e. the first question in this example is about the space station, not a random Apollo mission, because just about everyone knows a little about the ISS)*  
* *Sports trivia*  
* *Superlatives (i.e. “The worlds heaviest pumpkin: Over/under 1000 lbs?”)*  
  *It is **CRUCIAL** to pick a number rather close to the actual number so that it’s a difficult guess.*

# Round 4 – The List Round

*In this round, instead of asking three questions, I will ask contestants one question. Their answer will come in the form of a list. They will get one point for each answer They get correct.*   
   
*For example, if I said “name all five Spice Girls” and the only answers They could give were “sporty and posh” then They would get two of the possible five points.* 

*Things that make good list rounds:*

- *WELL KNOWN groups of 5-10 items. The more items in a list, the better known the group should be. A 10 item list needs to be an very commonly referenced group, like the planets in our solar system*  
- *A group of celebrities \*with a key commonality like genre of art/music, familial relation, etc\* across a range of ages, and we ask the contestants to sort them by age. \[EX: ordering 5 members of the Kardashian family by age is a good list round, but ordering even 4 members of Benjamin Franklin’s family is too obscure of a task.*  
- *We can also choose from a category with far more than 5-10 entities, and ask for the top \~5. For example, “Name the top 5 billboard artists from the year 2010” or “Name the top 6 countries in the world by population)*

#  Round 5 –Game Show Style (4 points each)

*This round varies heavily from week to week. Formats possible:*

1. *“To tell the truth” – simply three True/False questions. They can all be true (or all be false) sometimes, but only about 35-40% of the time. These can be T/F in the original question or other questions/factoids turned INTO T/F questions.*   
2. *“Name that tune” – a list of 2 earworms with lesser-known names or one-hit-wonder artists (eg My Sharona), and one “gimme” easier (more popular) song. Contestants need to provide the title AND artist for each*  
3. *“Who wants to be a millionaire” – 3 hard difficulty trivia questions, but with multiple choice answers generated. You can occasionally (25% of the time) have the “wrong” multiple choice answers be very obviously wrong, allowing players to figure out the correct answer by process of elimination, but this should also coincide with a rather challenging trivia question.*   
4. *“Family Fued” – we take a survey question directly from the game show, contestants need to supply the \#1 answer to that question. Always include the top 10 for my reference, laid out in a 2col / 5 row table.*

Round 7 – Mixing Things Up (5 points each)  
*Again, lots of variance in this round. Feel free to do any of the below:*

- *“Who am I?” – Classic jeopardy style questions about famous/notable individuals. They can have a theme (like “Famous women”) but it shouldn’t be too obscure (like “Famous women of the 1940s”), since we want these to generally be medium difficulty. You can also have NO theme/commonality and simply ask about any 3 notable individuals.*   
- *“Size matters” – Name two tall landmarks/famous buildings. Contestants need to answer which is taller.*  
- *“name that \[x\] brand.” Name 2 products of a brand; contestants answer name of the brand. (i.e. “Q: Aventador and Huracan / A: Lamborghini”)*  
-  *“Name that sports team” Name a US university/city; contestants answer with the name of the teeam associated with that University/city. Keep questions/answers all within the same league. I.E. either all college sports or all major league sports. Do not reference minor league sports.*
